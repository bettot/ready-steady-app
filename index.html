Keep everyone steady
Ten games for when the power is out the fun part
  • Shadow theatre — one torch, one wall, everyone's hands.
  • The listening game — sit in the dark 60 seconds; who heard the most?
  • Candlelight story circle — one sentence each, build a story.
  • Torch hide-and-seek — seeker gets the torch.
  • The tasting game — blindfold + five pantry items to identify.
  • Card tournament — keep a deck with the emergency supplies.
  • Blanket fort night — the warm room becomes the fort. Also heat strategy. Don't tell the kids.
  • Radio DJ — kids "present" the news from the wind-up radio.
  • Memory lane — adults tell stories from before phones. Kids find these unbelievable.
  • The gratitude round — before sleep: one good thing from today, even today.
Talking with kids — without the scary parts by age
Children take their cue from your tone, not your words. Calm voice, simple facts, always end at safety.
Ages 3–6

Keep it concrete and cosy: "Sometimes the lights go out. Then we light our lanterns. Want to see our special cupboard?" Give them a job — torch keeper counts.

Ages 7–12

Ask first: "What have you heard?" Correct gently, then anchor: "That's exactly why we have our plan." Show them the app. Kids this age become its biggest fans.

Teens

Give them the real thing — respect is the reassurance. Put them in charge of something real: the power banks, the pet plan, teaching the little ones a game.

If they ask "will something bad happen to us?"

"Probably not. But our family likes being ready, because being ready means it would be okay. That's why it's called a just in case."
